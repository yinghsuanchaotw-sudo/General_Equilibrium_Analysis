Model assumptions & classroom discussion

Flexible prices, a closed economy, lump-sum taxes, and fixed expected inflation. We solve L̄ → Ȳ → r → P. The animation explains equilibrium comparisons; it is not a calibrated timeline. Interest-rate effects on labor supply are omitted.

Temporary productivity: labor supply and expected future productivity are fixed. Permanent productivity: a wealth effect reduces labor supply; higher current labor demand dominates here. Consumption smoothing offsets the output effect on saving; stronger investment raises r. The price response in this case depends on the strengths of the income and interest-rate effects.

The hurricane reduces current capital and labor supply. Rebuilding raises investment in the illustrated case; the wage effect depends on the relative labor-demand and labor-supply shifts.

Higher government purchases are financed with current lump-sum taxes; the negative wealth effect raises labor supply. The non-Ricardian tax cut raises perceived wealth, consumption, and leisure. Under Ricardian equivalence, households save the tax cut to cover future taxes; national saving and real outcomes do not change.

Money demand is D(Ȳ,r + πᵉ). Higher Ȳ shifts it right; higher r reduces desired balances along it. P = Mˢ / D. When both Ȳ and r rise, the sign of P can depend on response sizes. Monetary shocks change Mˢ while real outcomes remain fixed in this model.
